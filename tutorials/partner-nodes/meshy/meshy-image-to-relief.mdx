The Meshy Image to Relief Partner Node turns one front-facing photo into a bas-relief mesh and a 16-bit depth map. Relief height follows the photo's brightness, so dark artwork on a light background comes out raised. That makes it a tool for 3D printing, laser engraving, and CNC carving rather than a way to model an object in the round.

## Set up the piece

1. Load a front-facing image. Put a dark subject on a light background, and flatten any transparency onto white first
2. Choose `relief_type`: `low` carves 3 mm for laser engraving, CNC, and plaques, `high` carves 6 mm for 3D-printed pieces
3. Set `size_mm` for the longer side of the photo on the model (20 to 250 mm), and pick `emboss` or `engrave`
4. Choose a `base`: `plate` adds a backing plate with `base_thickness_mm`, `none` cuts the background away
5. Set `bend` to `off`, `convex`, or `concave` to wrap the relief around a cylinder
6. Connect the outputs you need, then click **Run**

## Outputs

Connect only what you need. Meshy generates and downloads the formats wired to the outputs: GLB, STL, OBJ, FBX, USDZ, 3MF, the depth map, or a preview render.

- The depth map is 16-bit. Save it with **Save Image (Advanced)** as a 16-bit PNG for a laser or CNC height map
- `mesh_resolution` runs from 512 to 2048 and sets the mesh density. At 1024 the GLB is about 50 MB, and a bend roughly doubles it
- `color_mapping` colors the GLB, OBJ, FBX, and USDZ from the photo. STL and 3MF stay white

## Limits

- Height comes from brightness: a busy or dark background flattens the relief
- One artwork per call, front elevation only. To build an object in the round, use [Meshy 7](/tutorials/partner-nodes/meshy/meshy-7) image to 3D instead
- The seed only decides whether the node re-runs. Results are not reproducible
