**Bria Product Holding** takes a photo of a person and one to three photos of a product, then renders the person holding that product. **Bria Virtual Try-On** does the same with garments and accessories: each item replaces the matching item the person already wears and is refitted to the body.

Both nodes keep the person's face, pose, and background. The scene is re-rendered at about one megapixel, so a phone snapshot plus a product shot is enough for a lifestyle image or a catalog look without a photoshoot.

They are cloud API nodes: they need a Comfy account with credits, download no model files locally, and return one image that you write with a Save Image node.

## Available workflows

### Product Holding

Place a product into the hands of the person in the photo. The node starts with one product socket; the plus button on the node adds sockets for up to three images of the same product, such as the product itself, its packaging, another angle, or a companion item.

### Virtual Try-On

Dress the person in a garment or accessory from a separate product shot. Each connected item replaces the matching worn item, and two garments are combined into one layered outfit, so a top and a skirt can go into the same run instead of two runs.

## Node controls

| Control | Values | Applies to | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `person_image` | one image | both nodes | The photo the product or garment is placed onto. The face, pose, and background are kept |
| `product_images` | 1 to 3 images | Product Holding | Images of one product. A batched input counts once per image, and transparent areas are treated as white |
| `garment_images` | 1 to 3 images | Virtual Try-On | Garments or accessories worn together, each replacing the matching worn item |
| `instruction` | text, optional | both nodes | Extra direction on top of Bria's built-in one, such as `hold the bag in one hand` or `roll the sleeves up` |
| `aspect_ratio` | `auto`, `1:1`, `2:3`, `3:2`, `3:4`, `4:3`, `4:5`, `5:4`, `9:16`, `16:9` | both nodes | `auto` keeps the framing of the person photo. A fixed ratio returns 0.6 to 1 megapixel, for example 768x1024 at 3:4 |
| `seed` | integer, fixed by default | both nodes | Change it to explore variations of the same inputs |
| `moderation` | `true`, `false` | both nodes | Turns the visual output moderation toggle on |

## Prompting tips

- **Start from empty hands.** A person photo with visible, empty hands gives the model room to place the product. A closed fist or an occupied hand usually leads to an awkward result.
- **Keep the product shots consistent.** One to three images of the same product work best: the front, the packaging, and one alternate angle. Mixing two different products splits the render.
- **Leave `instruction` empty first.** The built-in direction handles most cases. Add a line only for a specific pose or face, such as `hold the bag in one hand` or `face the label to the camera`.
- **Use a garment shot, not a full outfit.** A hanger shot or flat lay of the single item is what gets refitted to the body. A whole look in one image tends to replace more than intended.
- **Layer garments in one run.** Two connected garments become one outfit, which keeps the light and the fit consistent across the pieces.
- **Keep `aspect_ratio` on `auto` for a matching edit.** It preserves the framing of the original photo, so the before and after line up.
